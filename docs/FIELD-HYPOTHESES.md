# Field-verification hypotheses and model audit

As of 2026-10-30 · v0.7.0

Five hypotheses deserve targeted field checks; none is investment-ready. Puyallup and Ronald Reagan are first investigation priorities; Elgin requires an access screen, while Edmonds and Medford require an existing-project redesign scope. The selected pool is not a national optimum.

Qualitative diligence order, not a return ranking or probability. Based on retail/access evidence, frozen nearby supply, address certainty and a concrete verification route. Five selected from the existing 37; the other 32 were not proven unviable. No new nationwide inventory sweep.

## What changed

### The shared downside is not a site forecast

The $51,392 revenue versus $60,000 fixed-cost comparison is correct. The error would be using that uncalibrated bundle to rule out real sites. Keep it as a historical stress test and investigate distinct local hypotheses. 

### Four-city paybacks are projections

The reported 4–7 year results were media tests of Tesla’s configurator, not measured realized project returns. They are useful vendor comparisons with different growth, rent, cost and horizon assumptions. [V7-ELECTREK-CITIES](https://electrek.co/2026/04/08/tesla-supercharger-for-business-configurator-pricing/)

### A better energy anchor, with limits

The live Tesla tooltip states approximately 35 kWh per session. We use kWh per post per calendar day as the main demand unit; 35 only converts units. Neither that average nor the US network usage claim establishes local paid demand. [S53158f4](https://www.tesla.com/supercharger-for-business/get) · [S1a5269e](https://www.tesla.com/learn/supercharger-for-business)

### Demand charges cannot be counted twice

The vendor utility field already includes demand and other energy charges at a fleet-based load profile. Higher use may dilute demand charges, but that is not an extra discount on the all-in input; a local interval/tariff model must replace it. [S580a7b3](https://www.tesla.com/learn/higher-supercharger-site-utilization-increases-profits) · [S53158f4](https://www.tesla.com/supercharger-for-business/get)

It is not a matched local analogue, a demand ceiling or evidence that utilization always rises. Tesla uses 7% yearly utilization growth, no parking rent and a 15-year illustration, with hardware taxes excluded. The analysis below does not reproduce that calculator: it uses flat demand, construction/ramp, explicit uncovered-cost allowances and a 10-year horizon. No growth is assumed merely because a network grew.

## Frozen hypothesis method

These are vendor-anchored working hypotheses, not calibrated forecasts. Demand factors 0.7/1/1.2, capital factors 1.35/1.2/1.1, utility factors 1.25/1/1 and monthly non-utility costs $2,000/$1,000/$500 were fixed before outcomes. Construction is 1.5/1/0.75 years; first-year ramp 50/75/100%; replacement 2/1.5/1% of gross capex. All cases use four posts, flat real demand, no grant, 10 operating years and the unchanged 6/8-year and 10% NPV gates. These joint stresses have no assigned probability.

The 15% loss input is a working grid-to-billed-energy assumption borrowed from Tesla’s different grid-to-battery disclosure. Displayed prices are not verified net receipts. The $470k medium-install illustration excludes hardware tax and does not establish complete utility, civil, land or financing scope. Contingencies, fixed costs and gross-asset reserves are unquoted; reconcile uncovered costs with the $0.10/kWh service package. No inferred savings or credits are admitted. The 35 kWh/session value only converts energy units; it is not a local session forecast.

The 35 kWh/session divisor is an explanatory unit conversion. Cash flows are driven by the daily kWh anchor and are invariant to that conversion. The 70%/100%/120% cases are author-selected alternatives, not empirical confidence bounds. A field forecast must be calibrated using matched paid observations, seasonality, access and incremental capture.

## What would validate or stop a hypothesis

Require owner-approved site/parking/access rights; utility identity and a written capacity, design, tariff and commissioning offer; and a scope-matched EPC quote including grid works, civil/ADA/drainage, equipment, labor, permits, tax, contingency and uncovered O&M. A percentage allowance does not verify completeness. Reconcile covered equipment service against site/admin/insurance and replacement items.

For a new site, use comparable paid-energy records with actual available post-days, seasons, prices, downtime and competition, then validate local EV traffic, stopping need and capture without treating city registration share as roadway share. A seven-day observation can detect an access or competition problem; it does not establish an annual demand distribution. Reject the proposed scope when credible demand/cost/service evidence cannot clear the unchanged gates; otherwise refine the design and rerun the full paired scenarios. No outreach or fieldwork was performed.
