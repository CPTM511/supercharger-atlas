Yes: grants, temporary bill support, clean-fuel credits and commercial cofunding can change returns. Their ownership, eligibility, timing and expiry matter. ## Different owners have different cash flows

### Tesla-owned charging network

Charging receipts accrue to the charging operator; host terms are separately negotiated. Tesla bears project/operating costs under its own scope; public reseller equipment prices do not disclose its internal costs.

Keep the same investment gates, but do not label third-party illustrative P&L as Tesla’s return. [V6-a206ed3093](https://www.losgatosca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/35905/Staff-Report-with-attachments-Tesla-Amendment) · [V6-6e5fa645e3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828026066775/tsla-20260930.htm)

### Third-party ownership / Tesla service

Owner sets charging prices and receives remittances less contractual fees; other rights require contract confirmation. Owner buys hardware, contracts installation and pays electricity/site costs. Reconcile equipment service, card/internet fees already included.

The existing owner cash-flow framework fits this question, but regional stress inputs are not parcel forecasts. [S1a5269e](https://www.tesla.com/learn/supercharger-for-business) · [S53158f4](https://www.tesla.com/supercharger-for-business/get)

### Property host / landlord

Negotiated rent or evidenced incremental merchant margin; no automatic ownership of charging receipts or credits. Host obligations and parking opportunity cost are contractual; do not impose an entire charger capex by default.

Separate host economics cannot rescue the charging investor’s failed hurdle. No host contract obtained. [V6-a206ed3093](https://www.losgatosca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/35905/Staff-Report-with-attachments-Tesla-Amendment) · [S1a5269e](https://www.tesla.com/learn/supercharger-for-business)

- **WA LMD-FCI unused-capacity credits** Unused DCFC capacity can earn a separate five-year credit stream after approval. Applications are due by December 31, 2029. Equipment permitted before January 1, 2023 or required as SEPA mitigation is excluded. Public access, network reporting and commissioning are required. Regular dispensing credits remain distinct. No current candidate approval or equipment permit date is verified. Existing equipment cannot become newly eligible solely because the atlas proposes an investment. Expansion requires approval and does not restart an existing five-year term. Determine registered FSE boundaries, nameplate and effective simultaneous power for the actual cabinet/post design: eight nominal 250 kW posts must not be blindly counted as 2,000 kW of simultaneous supply. If approved eligible aggregate nameplate exceeds 1,500 kW, an exception is required; the August 25 table showed 44 larger-site equipment slots and 44 approved. Do not assume an exception, new credit clock or flat unused-capacity revenue at high utilization. For approvals after November 20, 2025, generation is subject to the rule’s cumulative-value condition: prior-quarter estimated capacity-credit value must be below 1.5 times initial capex excluding on-site generation, less external capital/operating/maintenance funding. The five-year clock begins in the approval quarter, not at commissioning. Agency value uses quarterly average prices and 10% annual discount to approval-year dollars; this is not guaranteed cash. ## What the new sensitivity calculates

Separate conditional sensitivities preserve the original gross costs and hard gates. Support is never booked as an actual award. Zero/30%/80% gross-capex cases are mathematical ceilings, not eligible-cost determinations. Payback is lasting cumulative recovery within the modeled horizon: temporary recovery followed by later losses does not count. Temporary Machias support expires and is capped; no other aid is stacked. The Machias illustration retains the old eight-post Tesla/state-electricity proxy; it is not a costed grant-compliant design. No observed site return has changed.

The app shows each existing conservative/base/optimistic scenario with 0%, 30% or 80% hypothetical reimbursement of gross initial capex. This deliberately generous ceiling does not imply that all gross costs are eligible or that a program offers these percentages. It then separately varies base-case demand to 4, 8, 10 or 12 sessions/stall/calendar day with the same chosen support. More volume is an assumption, not an observed candidate forecast. Neither table tests a newly validated downside demand case.

The original gross capital, operating costs, construction holding costs and gross-asset replacement reserve are retained. Reimbursement arrives at commissioning or at the end of the first operating year. It is not netted against initial construction funding, and its cash receipt is discounted at its actual modeled time. Financing interest, tax effects, application costs, clawbacks and conditional compliance expenses are not supplied by this sensitivity. Actual grant applications and a liquidity/financing plan may make results worse.

A capital grant cannot fix negative recurring operating cash. Clean-fuel credits, utility support and retail gross profit could affect recurring cash, but require eligible metering, contractual ownership, expiry, net realization and incremental-cost evidence. Apart from the explicitly conditional Machias illustration, these recurring mechanisms stay unquantified; no fabricated credit price or retailer revenue is admitted.
